Timing Correction:

The published methods defined early surgery as DHC on procedure day ≤1 (admission day = 0), but the code classified days 0–2 as early. All revised analyses use day 0–1 (early) vs day ≥2 (delayed), as stated in the methods. This changes the timing result: in the published model, delayed surgery was not associated with mortality (0.84 (95% CI 0.65–1.07)), but with corrected timing it is associated with lower mortality (0.66 (95% CI 0.54–0.82)). The correction changed the statistical significance of the timing association for in-hospital mortality only; significance was unchanged for the other outcomes (all outcomes in Appendix B3). Age results are essentially unchanged.

Revised Primary Model:

Corrected timing, corrected codes, recomputed Elixhauser count, and the covariates requested in Comment 1. In this model, delayed surgery: 0.67 (95% CI 0.54–0.82); age >70 years: 1.94 (95% CI 1.27–2.96). All sensitivity analyses below use this model, with in-hospital mortality as the outcome unless stated.

Response to Reviewers:

Comment 1: Reperfusion Treatment, Transfer Status and Year (Reviewers 1 and 3)

  • Reviewer Comment: Thrombectomy, IV thrombolysis, transfer status and calendar year are absent from the covariates.

  • What Was Done:

    • Mechanical thrombectomy: the 17 specified ICD-10-PCS codes, searched in all 25 procedure fields; code frequencies checked by year (Appendix B1); timing relative to DHC from matched procedure days.

    • IV thrombolysis: 3E03017, 3E03317, 3E04017, 3E04317 (index hospital) or Z92.82 (tPA at another facility within 24 hours), combined into one treatment-receipt indicator; the source is reported separately (Appendix B2).

    • Calendar year (categorical) and transfer status (TRAN_IN) added. The timing–mortality model was repeated in patients with TRAN_IN = 0, excluding missing transfer status.

  • Results:

    • Thrombectomy was recorded in 25.6% and IV thrombolysis in 20.1% of hospitalizations; 35.7% were transferred from another acute-care hospital.

    • 14 of the 17 thrombectomy codes occurred in the data, across all study years; stent-retriever codes (…3Z7) appeared from 2018. Where both days were recorded, thrombectomy preceded the DHC day in 80.9% of thrombectomy patients.

    • Adding these covariates did not materially change the age or timing estimates (table below). The timing association persisted in non-transferred patients.

Comment 2: Case-Definition Sensitivity Analyses (Reviewer 1)

  • Reviewer Comment: Secondary-position AIS diagnoses and non-specific cranial procedure codes were allowed, and concurrent intracerebral hemorrhage was not addressed.

  • What Was Done: We confirmed that the original code did not exclude intracerebral hemorrhage. Three restrictions were run separately, each keeping all other criteria:

    • A. Principal-diagnosis AIS: I10_DX1 begins with I63 (decimals removed, format normalised).

    • B. Restricted DHC: 00N00ZZ or 00N70ZZ in any procedure field; timing from the earliest day matched to these two codes.

    • C. Hemorrhage-excluded: no diagnosis beginning with I61. This may also remove true AIS with hemorrhagic transformation.

  • Results: The retained sample and estimates for each restriction are below; results for all outcomes are in Appendix B3.

    • The exploratory comparison included 1,872 patients (424 deaths).

Comment 4: Post-DHC Length of Stay (Reviewer 1)

  • Reviewer Comment: Total LOS includes the preoperative days used to define delayed surgery.

  • What Was Done: Post-DHC LOS = LOS minus the earliest DHC procedure day. Excluded: DHC day not recorded (56), DHC day before admission (≤10) and DHC day after LOS (0), unweighted. The adjusted LOS model was repeated with post-DHC LOS; total LOS is kept as a descriptive measure.

  • Results: Adjusted difference for delayed vs early surgery: total LOS 2.7 (1.0 to 4.3) days; post-DHC LOS -0.8 (-2.4 to 0.8) days.

Comment 5: Discharge Disposition Among Survivors (Reviewer 1)

  • Reviewer Comment: The non-home outcome combined death with discharge destination.

  • What Was Done: We confirmed that the published analysis counted routine discharge and home health care as home, and all other dispositions, including death, as non-home. The revised analysis is restricted to survivors (DIED = 0): home = routine discharge or home health care; non-home = transfer to a short-term hospital or to a skilled nursing, intermediate care or other facility. Discharges against medical advice and unknown destinations are excluded from the model. Sparse cells were checked before fitting.

  • Results:

    • Survivors: 1,983 (unweighted); ≤10 excluded from the model. The smallest home-discharge cell by age group or timing had 14 patients.

    • Non-home discharge among survivors: delayed vs early surgery, 0.92 (95% CI 0.65–1.32); age >70 years, 1.10 (95% CI 0.49–2.49). Full model in Appendix B4.

Comment 6: Age-by-Timing Interaction (Reviewer 3)

  • Reviewer Comment: Whether the timing–mortality association differs by age was not tested.

  • What Was Done: Age group × timing was added to the revised mortality model, with a joint design-based Wald test of the three interaction terms. Delayed vs early odds ratios are reported for each age group, whether or not significant.

  • Results: No evidence that the timing association differs by age (F = 0.47 on 3 and 1324 df, p = 0.704). Every cell had at least 39 deaths.

Comment 7: Methods Verification (Reviewers 1 and 3)

  • Reviewer Comment: More detail is needed on the survey design, Elixhauser measure, sample sizes, suppression and model stability, including age–payer overlap.

  • What Was Done and Results:

    • Survey Design: Hospitals (HOSP_NIS) as clusters, NIS_STRATUM as strata and DISCWT as weights, with nested clusters and the lonely-PSU adjustment, specified on the full 2017–2022 NIS. The analytic cohort was defined by subsetting the full design, so variance reflects the complete sampling design. Because NIS hospital and stratum identifiers are year-specific, the model was refitted with year-specific strata and hospital identifiers (table below); standard errors changed by a factor of 1.00 to 1.05.

    • Elixhauser: The original measure was an unweighted integer count (range 0–13). It was recomputed as an unweighted count of the 31 Elixhauser categories using the Quan et al. (2005) ICD-10 algorithm in the R comorbidity package (version 1.1.0), which agreed exactly with the original in 97.4% of hospitalizations (r = 0.997). The recomputed count is used in all revised models.

    • Sample Sizes and Suppression: Unweighted model sizes, events and complete-case exclusions are below; all models converged, with no separation. The largest standard errors came from Hospital location and teaching status: Urban, non-teaching, reflecting small categories rather than instability in the age or timing estimates. All suppression used unweighted counts.

    • Payer: Of patients aged >70 years, 222 of 254 had Medicare. The largest variance inflation factor was 2.22 (AgeGroup; Appendix B5). Without payer, delayed surgery: 0.67 (95% CI 0.55–0.82); age >70 years: 2.22 (95% CI 1.54–3.20) (table below). Because collinearity was low and the age and timing estimates were stable, payer and atrial fibrillation were retained. Results with private insurance as the reference are in Appendix B6.
